JOBFILTER — FULL WEBSITE + REPOSITORY AUDIT AND NEXT-STEPS EXECUTION PROMPT
Date: 13 July 2026
Repository: manazoid4/JobFilterV1
Production: https://jobfilter.uk

============================================================
EXECUTIVE VERDICT
============================================================

JobFilter has a commercially strong core idea: identify high-intent UK construction opportunities earlier than shared lead marketplaces, score them, assign them to a trade/territory, and deliver the best opportunities through WhatsApp.

The current website is visually distinctive and much stronger than a generic AI SaaS landing page. It has clear category positioning, memorable language, a low-friction free scan, founder pricing, free tools, and a concrete sample lead.

The largest risk is not design. It is trust and product-reality alignment.

The repository README states that only Contracts Finder is currently live and that Planning Data is the next source. The production homepage repeatedly states that JobFilter already scans planning approvals, energy data, property signals and council contracts, that leads arrive 3–5 days before Checkatrade/Bark, and that one trade receives exclusive postcode access. Those claims must either be fully implemented and evidenced or rewritten immediately as pilot/roadmap language.

This should now be treated as a trust-first productisation sprint rather than another redesign.

============================================================
MULTI-LENS AUDIT
============================================================

1. PRODUCT / FOUNDER LENS

Strengths
- Clear pain: tradespeople waste time chasing low-intent and heavily shared leads.
- Strong wedge: early public-data signals rather than recycled consumer enquiries.
- Good delivery channel: WhatsApp-first fits the target audience.
- Simple initial price: £39/month is easy to understand and one won job can justify it.
- Territory exclusivity can become a meaningful moat if implemented honestly.
- Free tools create useful top-of-funnel acquisition and SEO opportunities.

Weaknesses
- The product currently tries to sell too many adjacent services: JobFilter, Vantage, Vicinity and Codex. This dilutes the main promise before the core lead engine has proven retention.
- “One trade per postcode” is operationally complicated. Define whether exclusivity means postcode district, cluster, radius, lead category, first-refusal period or absolute exclusivity.
- There is no visible proof of lead volume, source coverage, conversion rate, freshness, false-positive rate or customer outcomes.
- The homepage presents mature-product language while the repository documents an early source-limited implementation.

Priority decision
- Make JobFilter’s paid product one thing: verified, explainable, timely opportunity signals for a selected trade and territory.
- Move Vantage, Vicinity and Codex into a secondary “Services” page or hide them until the core subscription converts and retains.

2. TRUST / CLAIMS / COMPLIANCE LENS

Critical issues
- Do not claim planning, EPC/energy, property and company-registration coverage as live unless every source is actively ingested in production.
- Do not claim “3–5 days before Checkatrade or Bark” without defensible measurement and a documented methodology.
- The testimonial “First planning alert I got covered a loft conversion…” must be a genuine, permissioned customer statement. Otherwise label it clearly as an illustrative outcome or remove it.
- “Founding 30 places” and territory scarcity must reflect real inventory and be enforced in the backend. Artificial scarcity will damage trust.
- The site says “budget-confirmed leads,” while public procurement/planning signals may only provide estimated bands. Use “estimated project value” unless budget is actually confirmed by a buyer.
- A planning approval is not automatically a lead and may have no reachable buyer/contact. Call it an opportunity signal until contactability and buyer intent are verified.

Required trust system
- Add a visible methodology page explaining every source, scoring factor, confidence level, update frequency and limitation.
- Label all sample data as “Illustrative sample — not a live customer enquiry.”
- Show source attribution and detection timestamps on every real signal.
- Separate three concepts in the UI: Signal, Verified Opportunity and Contactable Lead.
- Add a plain-English “What JobFilter cannot know” section.

3. CONVERSION / COPY LENS

What works
- The hero is memorable and targets a real frustration.
- “Free scan — no card needed” is a strong primary CTA.
- The sample lead helps users understand the output.
- The guarantee and simple price reduce perceived risk.

What to improve
- The homepage is too long and repeats the same claims many times.
- The main CTA competes with “Check my patch,” “How it works,” pricing, free tools and three add-on services.
- The hero is dense. The user should understand product, proof and action within one screen.
- “Quit working for ghosts” is distinctive but should be followed by a precise, credible subheading.

Recommended homepage structure
1. Hero: one factual promise + free postcode/trade scan.
2. Live or clearly labelled sample result.
3. How the data works, with source badges and limitations.
4. Why this differs from Checkatrade/Bark.
5. Pricing and territory rules.
6. Genuine proof/case study.
7. FAQ and final CTA.

Suggested factual hero direction
“Find public construction opportunities in your patch before they become crowded enquiries.”
“JobFilter monitors UK procurement and property signals, scores trade relevance and sends the strongest opportunities to WhatsApp.”

4. UX / DESIGN LENS

Strengths
- Distinct industrial visual system.
- Strong typography and high visual recall.
- Cards communicate score, trade, location and value well.
- Mobile-first WhatsApp positioning is appropriate.

Risks
- Excessive uppercase, heavy borders and dense sections increase cognitive load.
- Yellow/black urgency is used almost everywhere, weakening hierarchy.
- Sample and live content are visually too similar.
- The page lacks a calm trust layer: methodology, data source logos, privacy, proof and limitations.
- Accessibility needs validation: contrast, keyboard states, semantic headings, motion preferences and screen-reader labels.

Actions
- Reduce homepage length by 35–50%.
- Use one dominant CTA per section.
- Add persistent “Sample” or “Live” badges to every data card.
- Add empty, error, partial-data and no-coverage states to the scanner.
- Test at 320px width and with 200% zoom.

5. ENGINEERING / ARCHITECTURE LENS

Observed strengths
- Next.js app-router structure.
- TypeScript compile check exists.
- Supabase server client and service-role separation are documented.
- Waitlist route includes basic sanitisation and rate limiting.
- README documents routes, APIs and known limitations.

Technical debt
- package.json contains mixed stacks: Next.js, Vite, Express, React Router and Alpine.js. Remove dependencies not used by the production architecture.
- Package name remains “react-example” and version is 0.0.0.
- “lint” only runs tsc --noEmit; no ESLint, formatting, unit, integration or end-to-end test command is defined.
- No visible CI quality gate is documented.
- The waitlist endpoint returns ok:true even if Supabase is unavailable, which can silently lose customer submissions.
- Waitlist contact validation can return “unknown” while still storing the entry.
- Rate limiting may be instance-memory based; validate that it works across serverless instances.
- Radius is documented as a preference, not true geospatial filtering, yet customer copy can imply local precision.

Required engineering baseline
- Standardise on Next.js 16 + React 19 + TypeScript.
- Remove unused framework dependencies.
- Add ESLint, Prettier, Vitest and Playwright.
- Add GitHub Actions for typecheck, lint, tests and build.
- Add structured logs, request IDs and error monitoring.
- Make data-write failures explicit to the user and retryable.
- Implement durable distributed rate limiting.
- Add schema validation with Zod for every public endpoint.
- Add bot protection to public forms and scanners.
- Add source health monitoring and freshness alerts.

6. DATA / SCORING LENS

Current strategic gap
The scoring model is a major product asset, but users cannot see enough of how it works to trust it.

Required score design
- Split the score into explainable dimensions:
  - Source confidence
  - Trade fit
  - Geographic fit
  - Freshness
  - Estimated value confidence
  - Buyer/contactability signal
  - Competition risk
- Never imply exact confidence where data is missing.
- Store score version and factor breakdown for every signal.
- Backtest scores against outcomes: viewed, contacted, quoted, won, rejected and false positive.
- Let users give one-tap WhatsApp feedback to improve ranking.

7. SECURITY / PRIVACY LENS

Actions
- Confirm SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY is never available to client bundles.
- Audit all public API routes for authentication, authorisation, Zod validation, payload limits, abuse and error leakage.
- Do not return internal exception messages directly in production.
- Add Content-Security-Policy, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy and secure cookie configuration.
- Document lawful basis, retention and deletion for subscriber/contact data.
- Minimise personal data from planning/public records and avoid exposing homeowner details unnecessarily.
- Add dependency scanning and secret scanning in CI.

8. SEO / ACQUISITION LENS

High-leverage opportunity
Build indexable, genuinely useful landing pages around trade + city + opportunity type rather than generating thin AI pages.

Examples
- /electrician-leads-birmingham
- /roofing-tenders-manchester
- /planning-leads-for-builders
- /public-sector-contracts-for-electricians

Each page should include real source coverage, recent anonymised examples, methodology, local context, FAQ and a scanner CTA.

Free tools should each have a standalone URL, explanatory copy, save/share output and an optional email/WhatsApp result delivery flow.
